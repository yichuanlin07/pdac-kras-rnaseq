# Methods draft: differential expression and cell-line omission analysis

## Count data and sample structure

We analysed the corrected gene-level count matrix retained in the project repository. The matrix contained 28,395 Entrez gene identifiers and 17 RNA-seq runs from eight pancreatic cancer cell lines: HPAC, MiaPaca-2, Pa01C, Pa02C, Pa04C, Pa14C, Pa16C and PANC-1. Each cell line had a control siRNA condition and a KRAS siRNA condition; MiaPaca-2 had two KRAS-condition libraries. We matched count columns to sample metadata, checked that counts were nonnegative integers, and compared every column with its source featureCounts table and assigned-count total. Gene annotations were matched by Entrez identifier. The corrected KRAS M1 counts were retained. This analysis began from the existing count tables; alignment and read quantification were not rerun in this work.

## MiaPaca-2 library handling

Following the October 10 group discussion, we summed the two MiaPaca-2 KRAS-condition libraries gene by gene at the raw integer-count stage, before gene filtering or normalization. Local KRAS P5 corresponds to SRR24828471, BioSample SAMN35622210, public alias MP2_K2v2; local KRAS P5_v2 corresponds to SRR24828472, BioSample SAMN35622209, public alias MP2_K2. Their summed assigned-count total was 68,124,190. The control library was SRR24828470, BioSample SAMN35622211, alias MP2_SC. The archived ENA snapshot dated October 4, 2026 records different BioSample accessions and FASTQ MD5 values for the two KRAS libraries. These records do not establish whether they represent technical or biological repeats or the same biological material. We therefore treated the summed counts as one analysis observation under an explicit exploratory pooling assumption, rather than counting the two libraries as independent replicates. The original run-level tables were retained, together with the previous analyses that used either library separately.

## Differential expression

The full analysis contained 16 observations, comprising one control and one treatment observation for each cell line. Genes were retained if they had at least 10 counts in at least two observations. We fitted the additive design `~ condition + cell_line`, with Control as the reference level and Treatment representing KRAS siRNA. The treatment coefficient estimated a common cell-line-adjusted effect. Positive log2 fold change indicates higher expression after KRAS siRNA. The full design had rank nine and seven residual degrees of freedom. It did not include a treatment-by-cell-line interaction, and the available design did not provide within-cell-line replication.

Analyses used R 4.5.2, DESeq2 1.50.2, edgeR 4.8.2 and apeglm 1.32.0. DESeq2 used median-of-ratios size factors, negative-binomial dispersion estimation and Wald tests, with independent filtering at `alpha = 0.05`. Its primary effect column, `log2FoldChange`, was the model estimate before apeglm shrinkage. We separately calculated apeglm posterior effect estimates and posterior standard deviations; shrinkage did not supply the primary Wald-test P values. edgeR used TMM normalization, robust dispersion estimation, robust quasi-likelihood fitting and a quasi-likelihood F test for the treatment coefficient, followed by Benjamini-Hochberg adjustment. We retained edgeR v4's default `legacy = FALSE` fitting behaviour. The reported edgeR `logFC` used its default prior count of 0.125 for effect estimation; it was not an apeglm estimate. Reverse contrasts were checked to negate the reported effect and preserve the raw P value in both methods.

We selected genes after testing using adjusted P < 0.05 and absolute log2 fold change > 0.5. DESeq2 used its estimate before apeglm shrinkage; edgeR used its reported `logFC`. This selection did not change the zero-effect null hypothesis into an effect-threshold test. A separate audit applied the same selection to DESeq2 apeglm effects to show the consequence of that alternative definition. Counts and overlaps were calculated on the full retained gene universe, rather than the 14,200-gene subset previously used for comparison with the published study.

## Leave-one-cell-line-out analysis

We performed eight additional fits. Each fit removed both the Control and Treatment observations for one cell line. Omitting MiaPaca-2 also removed the pooled treatment observation containing both KRAS libraries. Each reduced dataset contained 14 observations from seven cell lines. Unused factor levels were dropped; every design had rank eight and six residual degrees of freedom. We reapplied the same count filter within each reduced dataset and re-estimated normalization factors, dispersions, effects and tests with the same model and software settings. Genes excluded by filtering were recorded as unavailable rather than assigned zero effects or P values.

We compared each omission fit with the full fit using effect correlations, direction agreement, absolute effect changes, and overlap of the post-test screened sets. We tabulated each gene's effect range, number of finite estimates, number of available adjusted P values, direction agreement and screen-pass count. Full-fit screened sets were fixed reference lists for the stability assessment. The descriptive joint list required full-fit selection by both methods, finite estimates in all eight omission fits in both methods, the full-fit direction in every fit, and simultaneous selection by both methods in at least seven of the same eight rounds. We also reported the stricter eight-of-eight list. These correlated fits were sensitivity analyses; their P values were not pooled or averaged, and no additional error-control guarantee was assigned to the descriptive stability lists. A maximum absolute change of at least 0.5 log2 units was flagged for inspection, without treating this display rule as evidence of single-cell-line causation. No pathway enrichment, patient-outcome validation or new experimental work was performed in this analysis.
